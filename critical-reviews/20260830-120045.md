## Scope and decision

This pass asks whether a backward or forward UTC wall-clock adjustment can violate action, attempt, observation, event, projection, browser, or cooldown contracts. It follows the natural 0.86-second WSL rollback that reproduced finding 8 in the previous review. Monotonic duration measurement and browser-session expiry are reviewed separately from persisted UTC event time.

The decision is patch-after-review. Record timestamps may reflect UTC adjustments only where ordering is not contractual. Lease/deadline/cooldown authority must remain store-owned, DTO time ranges must remain constructible, retries must not wedge, and persisted ordering must have a deterministic non-clock tie-break where equal or regressed event time is allowed.

## Findings

### Finding 1: medium, UTC rollback invalidates action attempts after remote work

Status: closed.

The worker sampled `started_at`, observation, attempt end, retry, and completion independently. A one-second rollback after final dispatch made `ActionAttempt` reject `ended_at < started_at`; the generic failure handler then constructed the same invalid range and escaped without attempt or completion. Canonical ingestion may already have committed, leaving the action running until lease recovery and permitting repeated remote work.

Repair: inject one worker record clock, use the store authority clock in composition, and clamp every per-attempt observation/end/completion sample to the attempt start. Reuse one ordered end for attempt plus completion and derive retry from that end.

### Finding 2: medium, a later accepted Databricks result can lose projection ordering

Status: closed after explicit-provenance re-review.

Databricks currently has no source event timestamp, so its normalizer used local worker UTC for both `observed_at` and `received_at`. A later run after rollback could be accepted durably yet lose to the prior projection because the lower observation time is compared before receipt time.

Repair: SQLite assigns a strictly increasing durable receipt time inside the ingestion transaction. Databricks batches whose observation and receipt are the same local sample are treated as local-time evidence: when that sample regresses behind the latest durable receipt, both ordering fields advance to the new logical receipt. Exact replay reconstructs the stored ordering fields before digest comparison. A received-time index keeps restart and ingress lookup bounded.

Initial re-review found that the first implementation rewrote receipt time for non-local/source-timestamp batches as well, allowing a stale equal-observation delivery to win. Logical rewriting is now limited to the current Databricks local-time shape (`observed_at == received_at`); all other batches preserve both supplied fields. The existing equal-observation stale-delivery regression and the new local rollback/restart/replay regressions pass together.

### Finding 3: medium, raw UTC jumps extend or prematurely expire authoritative leases

Status: closed after mixed-duration restart re-review.

Although caller timestamps no longer own lease decisions, the store's default authority sampled raw UTC each time. Backward adjustment extended lease validity and delayed reclaim; forward adjustment expired live leases early.

Repair: default process authority anchors UTC once and advances it by `time.monotonic()` elapsed time. Every authority source, including injected test clocks, is protected by a nondecreasing floor. Startup restores that floor from the latest durable receipt and from active lease issuance (`leased_until − lease duration`), bounding restart behavior without treating a future lease deadline as current time.

Initial re-review proved that inferring every coordinator issuance from `leased_until − 60s` breaks supported mixed lease durations. Migration 0023 now persists exact `lease_authority_at` for intent claims and safely requeues legacy in-flight scopes whose issuance cannot be reconstructed. Fixed-duration action leases retain bounded inference. A simultaneous 10-minute/60-second restart regression proves both leases stay current at restart, only the short lease expires at one minute, and the long lease remains valid.

### Non-findings

- Action and operational-event cursor queries use stable ID tie-breakers; regressed display timestamps may sort older but do not break pagination.
- Browser capability expiry uses `time.monotonic()` and is not reopened.
- Dashboard `loaded_at` and freshness wall time are display snapshots, not durable authority.
