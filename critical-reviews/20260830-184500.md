Backend truth and remote behavior are outside this pass except where their state labels are rendered. The frontend-product workflow owns rendered verification; no visual redesign or generated imagery is justified.

## Findings

### F1 — Medium: focus indicators miss the 3:1 non-text contrast boundary

Every focus-visible rule used `color-mix(... var(--focus) 55%, transparent)`. Composited against the white and paper surfaces, the ring measures only 2.35:1 and 2.18:1. Its three-pixel geometry is clear, but the exact color does not meet the focus-indicator contrast threshold.

Repair: use solid `--focus: #126fa3`, which measures 5.49:1 on white and remains above 5:1 on the paper surface. Apply it consistently to text inputs, selects, buttons, links, and focusable scroll regions; protect both the exact source and computed ratio.

### F2 — Medium: all alert containers visually imply error severity

Alert badges already distinguish `info`, `warning`, `error`, and `critical`, but every `.alert-row` used the same red border and pink error background. Informational and warning records therefore carried contradictory row-level severity at scan speed.

Repair: render a severity modifier on each row and give info, warning, error, and critical coherent borders/backgrounds. Critical adds a dark inset edge; visible badge text remains the non-color cue.

### F3 — Low: valid ready/presence/expiry states relied on the generic badge fallback

`ready`, `present`, `absent`, `unknown`, and `expired` remained legible but had no explicit semantic selector. Add them to the active/success or neutral groups so the complete current vocabulary is intentional and future CSS changes cannot silently reclassify them.

### F4 — Medium: unfocused form controls have insufficient boundary contrast

White controls used the general `#d8dfdc` container border: 1.35:1 against white and 1.26:1 against the paper background. On the white authorization card, that border is the only field boundary.

Repair: add a dedicated `--control-line: #7f8d88` token and use it for every text input and select. It measures 3.46:1 on white and 3.21:1 on paper; the lighter line remains appropriate for passive containers.

### F5 — Medium: disconnected snapshot retains success-green presentation

Unavailable copy suppressed false data claims, but the banner and status dot retained the healthy green snapshot class immediately above the red disconnected notice.

Repair: render a disconnected modifier with danger-soft background, border, marker, and explanatory text. Healthy and worker-degraded snapshots retain green; negative render assertions protect the split.

### F6 — Medium: intent rows render red “None” and hide expiration reasons

Every “Failure” cell used danger styling even when empty. Expired scope disposition `request_expired` was omitted because composition exposed durable reasons only for failed/rejected states; SSR and polling also displayed underscore state labels inconsistently.

Repair: rename the field “Disposition detail”; use subtle styling for no failure, danger only for failed/rejected, and warning for expiry/cancellation detail. Expose expired/cancelled durable reasons, normalize underscores during SSR/polling, move expiry to warning and coalescing to active blue, and toggle classes during polling. A real expired-intent composition regression protects the durable reason.

### F7 — Low: recovery and alert wording carried avoidable false urgency

Worker-degradation reasons used danger red despite a warning-level parent state, and “Alertable failures” included informational events. Use warning text for disabled explanations and “Operational alerts” for the mixed-severity history.
